## Security

### What happened
A serious Windows zero-day was publicly released today by a disgruntled researcher, handing attackers a path to full System-level privileges. Coming on the same day as the Jensen Huang/Chinese spy scandal and ongoing chip smuggling revelations, today was a particularly bad day for the security posture of the U.S. tech ecosystem.

### Key stories
- **Windows "SignDrop" zero-day dropped publicly, granting System-level rights** — A disgruntled security researcher publicly disclosed a Windows zero-day exploit, bypassing responsible disclosure norms and immediately giving malicious actors access to a technique that grants local users all-powerful System privileges. The fact that this was an intentional leak — rather than an in-the-wild discovery — highlights the fragility of the researcher-vendor disclosure relationship and the real costs of that breakdown. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/windows-0-day-dropped-by-disgruntled-researcher-hands-hackers-system-rights/))
- **Reverse engineering consumer IoT to escape the cloud** — A Hacker News-highlighted post detailed a developer's work to reverse engineer a Coway air purifier and remove its cloud dependency entirely, reflecting a growing grassroots movement to reclaim local control over IoT devices. This matters at scale: millions of consumer IoT devices with cloud dependencies represent a significant and largely unaddressed attack surface. ([Hacker News / scottjg.com](https://scottjg.com/posts/2026-11-11-reverse-engineering-my-coway-air-purifier-to-ditch-the-cloud/))

### What to watch
Microsoft will need to rush an emergency patch for the SignDrop vulnerability given its public disclosure; watch for an out-of-band security update. The researcher's motivations for going public without coordinated disclosure will also be important to understand — it may reflect a broader breakdown in Microsoft's bug bounty or researcher relations program.
