- **Amazon Blocks Muse; Stratechery on Amazon's AI Moat** — Amazon has blocked Muse from its platform, a predictable defensive move, but Stratechery argues there is still room for a negotiated deal because Amazon's physical-world logistics and fulfillment infrastructure constitutes a genuine AI moat that Meta cannot replicate. ### What to watch
Whether Meta patches Muse's zero-day quickly and transparently will be a major early trust test for personal AI agents broadly. Longer term, watch for whether Walmart formally aligns with Meta as a distribution partner — that deal would materially change the competitive dynamics against Amazon.

## Cybersecurity: AI-Assisted Attacks & Platform Vulnerabilities

### What happened
Cybersecurity is under pressure from two directions simultaneously: AI is enabling adversaries to industrialize attacks at scale, and the new generation of highly privileged AI agents is introducing novel attack surfaces. Microsoft disrupted an AI-assisted mass compromise platform, Muse's zero-day revealed the dangers of agent privilege, and Radicle disclosed a vulnerability in its network protocol. The old perimeter-based security model is visibly inadequate for this threat environment.

### Key stories
- **Microsoft disrupts AI-assisted platform that compromised 12,000 accounts** — Microsoft disrupted EvilTokens, an end-to-end AI-assisted platform that made mass account compromises faster and easier for attackers, having already hit 12,000 accounts. This is a landmark case illustrating that AI capability diffusion is not only a productivity tool for legitimate users — it is an attack multiplier, and defenders need to assume adversaries have AI-assisted capabilities as a baseline. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/microsoft-disrupts-ai-assisted-platform-that-compromised-12000/))
- **Muse, Meta's extraordinarily privileged AI assistant, has a serious 0-day** — A critical zero-day in Muse allows a simple ClickFix attack to fully hijack the agent, which has sweeping access to user data, communications, and accounts. Beyond the immediate patch priority, this story raises a durable architectural question: the power that makes personal AI agents valuable is precisely what makes their compromise catastrophic, and the industry does not yet have mature security models for highly privileged agents. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/muse-metas-extraordinarily-privileged-ai-assistant-has-a-serious-0-day/))
- **The old cybersecurity model is breaking** — TechCrunch notes that cybersecurity stocks are rising and VC investment is surging as concern over AI safety and rogue agents grows, reflecting investor recognition that the threat surface is expanding faster than existing defenses can adapt. The convergence of AI-assisted attacks, highly privileged AI agents, and legacy perimeter models is creating a structural security crisis that no single product category can address. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/video/the-old-cybersecurity-model-is-breaking/))
- **Radicle discloses vulnerability in its network protocol** — Radicle, the decentralized code collaboration network, disclosed a vulnerability in its network protocol, a reminder that open-source infrastructure projects carry security risks that can propagate broadly through the developer ecosystem. ([Hacker News / Radicle](https://radicle.dev/2026/09/23/disclosure-of-vulnerability-in-network-protocol))

### What to watch
The Muse zero-day patch timeline will be closely watched, but the broader issue — how to build security models for highly privileged AI agents — is an open research and product problem that will define a generation of cybersecurity investment. Watch for new agent-specific security frameworks from Microsoft, Google, and the broader research community.
