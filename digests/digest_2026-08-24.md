The debate reflects broader tensions around privatized surveillance infrastructure in public spaces. It's complicated** — TechCrunch examines the legal landscape around AI training on copyrighted literary works, finding that the situation is murky at best. ## AI in Education & Cognitive Science

### What happened
MIT Technology Review published two important pieces today challenging assumptions about AI's cognitive parity with humans: research shows children still dramatically outperform AI at language acquisition, and educators are still struggling to design coherent AI policies for classrooms.

### Key stories
- **Kids outlearn AI — and we still don't know why** — Despite billions of dollars and trillions of tokens, current AI systems still cannot match the speed and efficiency with which human children acquire language. Children learn from a fraction of the data, in noisy real-world environments, with far greater generalization — and researchers don't have a satisfying explanation for why. This finding is a fundamental challenge to the "scale is all you need" thesis and suggests current architectures may be missing something essential about how intelligence works. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/24/1141740/kids-machines-language-learning/))
- **How to encourage smarter AI use in the classroom** — MIT Technology Review's Making AI Work newsletter tackles the thorny question of AI policy in schools, where ad hoc bans have largely failed and blanket permissiveness creates its own problems. The article argues for nuanced, task-specific policies that help students learn to use AI as a tool rather than a crutch. Getting classroom AI policy right is one of the most consequential near-term challenges in education, given how rapidly AI tools are becoming embedded in students' workflows. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/24/1142630/ai-school-classroom-policies/))

### What to watch
Watch for whether the cognitive science research on children's language learning vs. AI sparks renewed interest in alternative AI architectures — neurosymbolic approaches, embodied learning, or developmental AI — that try to capture what LLMs appear to be missing.

## Power Infrastructure & Energy Tech

### What happened
Asianometry explored whether solid-state transformers — a technology first conceptualized in 1968 — are finally reaching viability, driven in large part by the surging power demands of AI data centers that are straining traditional electrical grid infrastructure.

### Key stories
- **Has the Solid State Transformer's Time Finally Come?** — Solid-state transformers use power semiconductors to replace the century-old iron-core transformer design, offering benefits like smaller size, digital controllability, and bidirectional power flow — capabilities that are increasingly valuable for integrating renewable energy and managing volatile data center loads. The technology has repeatedly failed to achieve commercial scale due to cost and reliability challenges, but the current moment — with AI driving unprecedented power demand growth and semiconductor costs falling — may finally be the inflection point. If SSTs do achieve commercial scale, the implications for grid modernization and data center power architecture could be profound. ([Asianometry](https://asianometry.passport.online/member/episode/has-the-solid-state-transformers-time-finally-come))

### What to watch
Watch for announcements from power electronics companies and hyperscalers investing in SST deployments, and monitor whether grid operators begin specifying SST capability in new infrastructure procurement.
