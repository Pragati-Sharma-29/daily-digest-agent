### Key stories

The claim is both a genuine technical achievement and a reputational land-grab that is deepening rifts with professional mathematicians who question the rigor and novelty of AI-generated proofs. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/994255/openai-millennium-prize-problem-tristan-buckmaster-competition))

The letter reflects deep anxiety in a community that had previously seen itself as largely insulated from AI disruption. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/openais-feud-with-mathematicians-is-only-escalating/))
- **A misalignment of AI in mathematics** — Hacker News surfaced a dedicated resource (mathandai.org) cataloging the concerns mathematicians have about AI's role in the field, reflecting a growing organized opposition to how AI labs are framing their mathematical achievements. This suggests the pushback is moving from individual complaints to a coordinated community response. ([Hacker News / mathandai.org](https://mathandai.org/))

### What to watch
Whether the Millennium Prize foundation or peer review bodies weigh in formally on OpenAI's claimed solution will be a major development — if the mathematical community rejects the claim, it would be a significant reputational blow and could set precedents for how AI-generated proofs are evaluated.

### Key stories
- **Y Combinator's Garry Tan wants US open-weight AI labs to 'distill' frontier models, too** — Tan's argument is that the U.S. needs competitive open-weight models that aren't derived from Chinese labs, and that distilling from American frontier models (like GPT or Claude) is the fastest path to that. This is a notable shift in the open-source AI debate — from a question of open vs. closed to a question of American vs. Chinese open models. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/y-combinators-garry-tan-wants-u-s-open-weight-ai-labs-to-distill-frontier-models-too/))

### What to watch

## Cybersecurity & Data Breaches

### What happened
Two major security incidents are dominating today: Revolut confirmed that attackers obtained customer data by submitting fake government requests — a sophisticated social engineering attack on fintech infrastructure — and ClickFix malware campaigns are going viral, successfully targeting both Windows PCs and Macs by exploiting user frustration with complex software workflows.

### Key stories
- **Revolut confirms customer data breach through fake government requests** — Attackers impersonated government officials to extract customer data from Revolut through what appear to be fraudulent legal data requests. Revolut has notified affected customers and relevant regulators, but the incident exposes a systemic vulnerability in how financial institutions respond to legal data demands — a vector that is increasingly being exploited. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/12/revolut-confirms-customer-data-breach-through-fake-government-requests/))
- **ClickFix attacks infecting PCs and Macs are going viral** — ClickFix attacks exploit a deceptively simple social engineering trick: convincing users they need to run a script to fix a problem, then executing malware. The technique is spreading because it requires minimal technical sophistication from attackers while exploiting the genuine complexity and friction users face getting things done on modern operating systems. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/clickfix-attacks-infecting-pcs-and-macs-are-going-viral/))

### What to watch
The Revolut breach methodology — fake government requests — is likely to be replicated against other fintech and financial institutions. Regulators and legal teams at major platforms need to urgently review their processes for verifying the authenticity of government data requests.
