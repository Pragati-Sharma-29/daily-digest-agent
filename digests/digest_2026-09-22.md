## Hardware & Semiconductors: Intel's Optane Post-Mortem

### What happened
Asianometry published a detailed retrospective on Intel's Optane (3D XPoint) memory technology — a product that was announced in 2015 with revolutionary promises and ultimately discontinued without achieving its potential. The timing is relevant as the industry evaluates new memory architectures for AI workloads.

### Key stories
- **Intel's Optane Memory: A Promise Unfulfilled** — Intel and Micron announced 3D XPoint in 2015 as the first new memory category in 25 years, promising performance between DRAM and NAND flash with far greater density and endurance than DRAM. Despite the technical promise, Optane failed to find a market: it was too expensive for mass-market storage, too slow for true DRAM replacement, and Intel's push to make it viable ultimately cost the company billions in losses before it was discontinued. The lesson — that technically superior products can fail if they don't fit cleanly into existing workflows or price tiers — is directly applicable to today's AI memory architecture debates. ([Asianometry](https://asianometry.passport.online/member/episode/intels-optane-memory-a-promise-unfulfilled))

### What to watch
With AI workloads creating unprecedented demand for high-bandwidth, low-latency memory, watch for whether new memory technologies (HBM4, CXL-attached memory, etc.) learn from Optane's mistakes by targeting specific, validated use cases rather than trying to replace existing memory tiers wholesale.
