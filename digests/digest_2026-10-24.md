([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/moonshot-curbs-hacking-ability-in-new-kimi-model-as-fears-grow-over-ai-powered-cyberattacks/articleshow/135162609.cms))

([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/mark-cuban-says-ai-hack-agents-are-like-cockroaches-but-can-be-controlled/articleshow/135163806.cms))

### What to watch

This is one of the most concrete data points to date on the political bias embedded in major AI models.

### Key stories
- **Forced to pick, Grok's AI model chooses Republicans. OpenAI's picks Democrats.** — The study found Grok overwhelmingly recommends Republican candidates while OpenAI's GPT favors Democrats across nearly 500 election races, with agreement in fewer than 10% of cases. This is a damning finding: two of the most widely used AI models are not neutral information tools but politically directional systems, with the direction depending on which company built them. The implications for AI use in voter information, political research, and civic tech are profound. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/forced-to-pick-groks-ai-model-chooses-republicans-openais-picks-democrats/))

### What to watch
Expect this study to generate regulatory attention, particularly in the EU and from the FEC, as election season intensifies. The more pressing question is whether AI companies will implement meaningful guardrails or continue to claim their models are "neutral" while evidence mounts that they are not.

## Cybersecurity & Open Source Infrastructure Risk

### What happened
Two separate stories today highlighted the fragility of the open-source infrastructure that underpins much of the internet. PHP — used by the vast majority of websites globally — nearly suffered a catastrophic security incident, and a disgraced certificate authority exited the TLS business after a key management failure that could have broken web trust at scale.

### Key stories
- **PHP-ocalypse: How two critical web bugs almost broke the internet** — Two critical vulnerabilities in PHP triggered a global emergency response, exposing the precarious state of one of the web's most foundational technologies. Making the situation more alarming: the PHP Foundation, which maintains the language, operates with just one full-time staff member and is chronically underfunded — a reminder that vast swaths of internet infrastructure rest on shockingly thin institutional support. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/php-ocalypse-how-two-critical-web-bugs-almost-broke-the-internet/))
- **Disgraced eMudhra drops out of TLS business after botched key signing ceremony** — eMudhra, a certificate authority that had already faced scrutiny, exited the TLS business following a ceremony failure that would have allowed minting of fraudulent web credentials for any site. Certificate authority failures are among the most dangerous trust infrastructure incidents possible — and the ease with which this nearly happened underscores ongoing weaknesses in the web PKI system. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/disgraced-emudhra-drops-out-of-tls-business-following-certificate-debacle/))
- **PHP Foundation 'under-funded' with one full-time staff member, director says** — The PHP Foundation's director publicly acknowledged that the organization is critically underfunded, with only one full-time employee supporting a language that powers a majority of the world's websites. This is a systemic risk hiding in plain sight, and the PHP vulnerability crisis this week makes the funding gap impossible to ignore. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/php-foundation-under-funded-with-one-full-time-staff-member-director-says/articleshow/135162044.cms))

### What to watch
Watch whether the PHP incident catalyzes meaningful funding commitments from major tech companies and cloud providers who depend on the language — similar to how the Log4Shell crisis accelerated open-source security investment. The broader open-source infrastructure funding problem needs a structural solution, not one-off responses to crises.
