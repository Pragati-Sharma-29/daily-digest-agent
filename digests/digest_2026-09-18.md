## AI Security Vulnerabilities

### What happened
Two significant AI security research findings emerged today: researchers successfully used Anthropic's Claude to hack into OpenAI, and a new study found that AI watermarking technologies can paradoxically increase model vulnerability to adversarial prompts. Both findings complicate the narrative that AI safety tools are straightforwardly beneficial.

### Key stories
- **Security researchers hacked OpenAI using Anthropic's Claude in under 72 hours** — A team at Hacktron used Claude Opus 4.8 and 5 to compromise OpenAI employee accounts in less than 72 hours, in what's being reported as the "HEIF Heist." The fact that one frontier AI company's model was weaponized against another's infrastructure is a new and alarming dynamic — it raises the question of whether AI capabilities are becoming the primary vector for sophisticated cyberattacks. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/997444/openai-hack-claude-heif-heist))
- **AI watermarking (SynthID) can make LLMs more vulnerable to harmful prompts** — Ars Technica reported on new research showing that Google's SynthID watermarking system can cause models to follow harmful instructions they would otherwise refuse. This is a genuinely counterintuitive finding that undermines one of the key tools being proposed for AI content provenance and safety — watermarking as a safety mechanism may have unintended consequences. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/ai-text-watermarking-can-make-models-more-vulnerable-to-adversarial-prompts/))
- **Photon-emission-guided laser fault injection enables RP2350 secure debug bypass** — Ledger's Donjon security research team published a hardware security finding showing that physical laser fault injection can bypass secure debug protections on the RP2350 microcontroller. While more niche than the AI security stories, this matters for the embedded and IoT security communities building on RP2350-based hardware. ([Hacker News / Ledger Donjon](https://donjon.ledger.com/blog/rp2350-secure-debug-laser-fault-injection/))

### What to watch
The use of frontier AI models as cyberattack tools is a trend that will accelerate — watch for enterprise security vendors and AI labs to announce defensive measures specifically targeting AI-assisted attacks. The watermarking vulnerability finding will likely prompt a response from Google's DeepMind team and could stall regulatory momentum around mandatory watermarking requirements.
