## Agentic AI

### What happened
Agentic AI systems are moving from hype to production deployment, with both practical tooling guidance and new safety frameworks emerging today. Google Cloud published prescriptive guidance on agent skill evaluation to prevent context window bloat, while MIT Technology Review spotlighted "reverse game theory" as a novel alignment technique. Google also released a new "Physical-Engineering's Last Exam" benchmark designed specifically to evaluate agents on hard engineering tasks.

### Key stories
- **"Reverse game theory" could keep AI agents in check** — MIT Tech Review explains that mechanism design — structuring incentive systems so AI agents are naturally motivated to follow rules — could be more robust than hard constraints. This matters because as agents become more capable, alignment-by-rule-following breaks down; incentive-compatible design could scale better. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/03/1147934/reverse-game-theory-mechanism-design-ai-alignment/))
- **Google Cloud introduces Physical-Engineering's Last Exam for agents** — Google's AI & Infrastructure team launched a new benchmark for evaluating AI agents on physical engineering tasks, filling a gap left by text-centric benchmarks. It signals Google's intent to position its agent infrastructure as production-ready for hard scientific and engineering domains. ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/introducing-physical-engineerings-last-exam-for-agents/))
- **Five rules for evaluating agent skills before they bloat your context window** — Google Cloud published practical developer guidance around SKILL.md evaluation, warning that agents tested superficially on laptops often fail at scale. The piece signals the industry's growing maturity around agent productionization, moving beyond prompt engineering to systematic skill validation. ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/evaluate-agent-skills-before-they-bloat-your-context-window/))
- **Leading in the agentic era: putting people first** — Google Cloud Consulting shared lessons from enterprise AI agent deployments, emphasizing that the shift from generative AI to agentic AI requires change management at the human layer, not just technical integration. The key insight: failed agent deployments are often people problems, not model problems. ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/consulting/lessons-for-adopting-ai-agents/))
- **Raising the quality of insights in agentic analytics** — MIT Tech Review covered how agentic AI is transforming enterprise analytics by automating not just visualization but the full insight-generation loop. The implication is that traditional BI vendors face serious disruption as agentic pipelines can close the loop from data to action without human intervention. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/02/1147660/raising-the-quality-of-insights-in-agentic-analytics/))

### What to watch
Watch whether Google's Physical-Engineering Last Exam gets adopted as an industry benchmark alongside existing coding and reasoning evals. Also track whether MIT's reverse game theory approach spawns any frameworks that labs formally adopt in their alignment programs.

- **IoT cyberattack hits supermarket refrigerators across Europe** — A cyberattack compromised commercial refrigeration systems in stores across Europe from Sweden to Belgium, forcing food disposal. While Italy says the impact was limited, this incident illustrates the growing vulnerability surface of industrial IoT infrastructure. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/italy-says-cyberattack-on-supermarket-fridges-had-limited-impact/articleshow/135501166.cms))
- **Postman silently auto-installs AI plugins and attempts to share API data** — A GitHub issue went viral revealing that Postman was silently installing AI plugins and attempting to exfiltrate API data, sparking backlash from developers.
