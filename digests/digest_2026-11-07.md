## Cybersecurity: Breaches, Vulnerabilities & AI-Powered Attacks

### What happened
This was an exceptionally bad week for cybersecurity, with major breaches at Chainalysis and Binance, a newly discovered Linux SSH vulnerability, and hundreds of AI-powered hacking tools found publicly exposed — all pointing to an escalating threat environment in which AI is now being weaponized by attackers.

### Key stories
- **Chainalysis says hackers stole credentials and tokens for all of its customers** — The blockchain analytics firm disclosed a "multifront cyberattack" that included AI agents and an on-path data interception, resulting in the theft of credentials and session tokens for its entire customer base. The use of AI agents in the attack is particularly alarming, as it signals that adversaries are now operationalizing AI for sophisticated, multi-vector intrusions at scale. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/chainalysis-says-hackers-stole-credentials-and-tokens-for-all-of-its-customers/))
- **Binance CFO departs following $6.3 billion hack** — The announcement of the CFO's departure to "pursue a new venture" comes barely more than a week after one of the largest cryptocurrency hacks in history. The timing raises questions about internal accountability and the stability of Binance's leadership at a critical moment when the exchange faces intense regulatory and security scrutiny. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/07/binance-cfo-is-leaving-to-pursue-a-new-venture-after-6-3-billion-hack/))
- **Hundreds of Artex AI hacking tools found publicly exposed** — Security researchers discovered hundreds of servers hosting the AI-powered penetration testing tool Artex openly accessible on the internet, allowing anyone — including malicious actors — to access the tooling with no authentication. This is a dual-use AI risk made concrete: tools built for legitimate security research are being misconfigured and left exposed at scale. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/hundreds-of-artex-ai-hacking-tools-found-publicly-exposed-to-the-web/))
- **"*sshole" vulnerability allows passwordless SSH into some Linux devices** — Researchers disclosed a vulnerability in certain configurations of Alpine Linux (and potentially other distributions) that allows attackers to SSH into devices without a password. The catchy name belies a serious security issue for any organization running affected Alpine configurations in containerized or embedded environments. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/vulnerability-allows-attackers-to-log-into-some-linux-devices-sans-password/))

### What to watch
The Chainalysis breach is particularly consequential — watch for downstream impacts on its customers, which include many major crypto exchanges and financial institutions. The use of AI agents in the attack should prompt a broader industry conversation about AI-augmented threat modeling.
