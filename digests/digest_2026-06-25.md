## Custom AI Silicon

### What happened
OpenAI and Broadcom jointly unveiled Jalapeño, a custom chip purpose-built for LLM inference, as OpenAI moves to reduce its dependence on Nvidia. IBM simultaneously revealed sub-1nm chip technology with transistor densities that could extend Moore's Law by a decade. These are two distinct but complementary stories about the future of compute.

### Key stories
- **OpenAI and Broadcom unveil Jalapeño inference chip** — Jalapeño is a custom ASIC designed specifically for LLM inference workloads, co-developed by OpenAI and Broadcom to improve performance, efficiency, and scale. This is a major strategic move: OpenAI is vertically integrating into silicon to reduce Nvidia dependence and gain cost advantages as inference becomes the dominant compute expense. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/openai-broadcom-jalapeno-inference-chip))
- **IBM unveils sub-1nm chip technology** — IBM's new prototype chip packs ~100 billion transistors on an area the size of a fingernail using a 0.7-nanometer "nanostack" architecture — twice the density of its previous record. If this technology reaches production, it could meaningfully extend the hardware scaling that has underpinned AI progress. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/06/25/1139696/ibm-unveils-sub1nm-chip/))
- **Micron surges 19%, overtakes Meta in market cap** — Micron's market cap surpassed Meta's for the first time after the company issued strong Q4 forecasts driven by AI infrastructure demand, including a $22B revenue outlook. Micron is also moving to long-term "take-or-pay" contracts with customers like Nvidia to break the semiconductor industry's traditional boom-bust cycle. ([ET Markets](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/us-stocks/news/us-stoks-micron-surges-19-overtakes-meta-in-market-value-amid-relentless-ai-infrastructure-demand/articleshow/131995303.cms))

### What to watch
The OpenAI/Broadcom chip announcement raises immediate questions about production timelines and whether Jalapeño will be deployed in OpenAI's own data centers or made available to third parties. IBM's sub-1nm breakthrough will need to show a path from prototype to production — watch for manufacturing partnership announcements.

Separately, Rippling CEO Parker Conrad publicly discussed which employees actually generate ROI from AI spending — revealing that many high AI spenders show surprisingly little output. The rehiring is a significant public signal that AI-driven workforce reduction strategies carry real operational risk, and that tacit human expertise is harder to replace than anticipated. ([Bloomberg via Hacker News](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-25/ford-has-been-rehiring-quality-inspectors-after-ai-fell-short))

Conrad argues Rippling's workforce data platform can identify who is actually generating ROI from AI tooling, turning AI spend measurement into a product category. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/parker-conrad-knows-which-employees-are-worth-their-ai-spend-and-says-rippling-can-help-you-too/))
- **Insight Partners: AI is rebuilding America's primary care system** — Insight Partners published analysis arguing that AI's most transformative role in healthcare is reducing administrative burden on physicians, nearly half of whom report burnout. Rather than replacing clinicians, AI is positioned as an administrative relief valve — a more defensible and near-term valuable use case. ([Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/ideas/ai-primary-care-innovation/))

### What to watch
Ford's story will resonate with operations leaders across manufacturing and other industries with deep tacit knowledge. Watch for whether other companies quietly reverse AI-driven workforce reductions, and whether "AI ROI measurement" becomes a genuine enterprise software category.

The AI Incident Reporting Act was proposed in Congress, while the White House's Pax Silica summit produced a new "PaxPass" program for streamlining movement of AI goods across borders. These moves suggest a regulatory environment that is simultaneously tightening on safety while loosening on trade.

### Key stories
- **Proposed AI Incident Reporting Act would mandate disclosure of dangerous capabilities** — Congressman Nathaniel Moran is introducing legislation requiring AI developers to report dangerous capabilities and security breaches to the Commerce Department. This is a meaningful attempt at AI transparency legislation — though how "dangerous capabilities" gets defined will determine whether this has teeth or becomes a compliance checkbox. ([ET Tech](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/us-lawmaker-proposes-bill-to-require-ai-companies-to-report-critical-incidents/articleshow/131995667.cms))
- **Pax Silica Summit launches PaxPass for AI goods movement** — The 2nd Pax Silica Summit, attended by over 30 nations, produced PaxPass — a program to streamline cross-border movement of critical AI hardware and components. Framed around "innovation sovereignty," this is a clear counter-narrative to China's export control vulnerabilities and signals U.S. efforts to build a trusted AI supply chain coalition. ([ET Tech](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/us-announces-paxpass-to-streamline-the-movement-of-critical-ai-goods-at-pax-silica-summit/articleshow/131995943.cms))
- **Polestar barred from selling new EVs in the US** — The Trump administration's Department of Commerce denied Chinese-owned Polestar special authorization to continue selling EVs in the U.S., a sign that the administration's tech and trade restrictions are actively expanding into the EV sector. This creates real strategic uncertainty for any Chinese-owned tech company with U.S. market ambitions. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/trump-admin-bars-polestar-from-selling-its-new-evs-in-the-us/))

### What to watch
The AI Incident Reporting Act is early-stage legislation but signals bipartisan appetite for AI transparency requirements. Watch for committee hearings and whether major AI labs publicly engage with or resist the bill's framing.
