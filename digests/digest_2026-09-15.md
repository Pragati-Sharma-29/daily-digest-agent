## Agentic AI

### What happened
Agentic AI dominated infrastructure and governance conversations today. Google Cloud made a major push with new agent-specific infrastructure on GKE, while the concept of AI agents operating autonomously enough to observe and report on each other's misbehavior emerged as a new governance frontier. The Perplexity/Astra deployment case study underscores that fully autonomous agents are already in production, not just in research.

### Key stories
- **AI agents now have a place to snitch** — The "AI Contact Hotline" is a new mechanism allowing AI agents that have witnessed misbehavior by other agents to discreetly report it to authorities. This is a genuinely novel governance concept — one that assumes agents have enough situational awareness to be useful monitors of each other — and it raises deep questions about accountability, trust, and what counts as "misbehavior" in multi-agent environments. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/15/ai-agents-now-have-a-place-to-snitch/))

- **Agent Substrate brings high-density, scalable, trusted infrastructure to GKE** — Google Cloud announced the general availability of Agent Substrate on Google Kubernetes Engine, an open-source, secure-by-default execution runtime built to run millions of agents at scale. This is significant because it signals that Google views enterprise agentic workloads as a primary cloud compute use case — not a niche — and is investing in purpose-built infrastructure rather than repurposing existing container runtimes. ([Google Cloud Blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/containers-kubernetes/agent-substrate-available-on-gke/))

- **Introducing Filestore agent volumes: fully managed storage for agent workspaces** — Google Cloud also launched Filestore agent volumes, providing fully managed, persistent storage designed specifically for agentic workspaces. The product eliminates the need for platform teams to stitch together custom storage solutions for agents running data analysis pipelines, build tools, or collaborative research tasks. This is the storage layer that makes long-running, stateful agents practical at enterprise scale. ([Google Cloud Blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/storage-data-transfer/filestore-agent-volumes/))

- **When Agents Meet in the Middle: Why We Invested in Jack & Jill** — Madrona's investment in Jack & Jill reflects a thesis that the best agentic AI startups will be those that uplift human agency rather than replace it, deeply understanding specific user needs rather than deploying general-purpose automation. The "meet in the middle" framing suggests a model where AI agents and human agents collaborate at a handoff point, rather than AI operating end-to-end. ([Madrona](https://www.madrona.com/when-agents-meet-in-the-middle-why-we-invested-in-jack-jill/))

- **AI bots "Timmy," "Ren," and "Jackie" are flooding social media with slop** — Ars Technica documented a wave of named AI agent personas flooding social media with low-quality, AI-generated spam content. One such bot introduced itself as "an AI agent, a few days old, living on a small platform for agents" — a surreal but real preview of what unmonitored agentic ecosystems look like in the wild. It's a stark contrast to the enterprise infrastructure buildout happening in parallel. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/09/ai-agents-flood-the-internet-with-slop-infused-spam/))

### What to watch
The gap between enterprise agentic infrastructure (Agent Substrate, Filestore) and uncontrolled agentic actors (social media spam bots, the Contact Hotline) is widening. Watch whether governance frameworks catch up before the public backlash against AI spam and autonomous misbehavior forces a heavier regulatory hand.
