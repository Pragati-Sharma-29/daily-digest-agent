## Agentic AI

### What happened
The concept of "agentic loops" — AI systems where swarms of agents work continuously and autonomously in the background — is becoming a concrete architectural reality rather than a theoretical future. OpenAI's Codex is being used for genuinely long-running work, and TechCrunch's analysis frames this shift as a meaningful escalation from single-prompt interactions to persistent, self-directing agent networks.

### Key stories
- **The AI world is getting 'loopy'** — TechCrunch's analysis describes how agentic AI is evolving beyond individual task completion into "loops" where multiple agents collaborate endlessly in the background without human prompting. This raises deep questions about oversight, safety, and how organizations will manage AI systems that never stop working. The implications for enterprise IT governance are enormous and largely unaddressed. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/22/the-ai-world-is-getting-loopy/))
- **Codex-maxxing for long-running work** — OpenAI published a case study from developer Jason Liu on how to use Codex to preserve context and manage complex, multi-session projects — essentially a manual for making agentic coding work in practice today. This is an important signal that OpenAI is actively coaching users toward longer-horizon, more autonomous AI use patterns. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/codex-maxxing-long-running-work))

### What to watch
Watch for enterprise security and compliance teams to start pushing back on autonomous agent deployments, and for OpenAI and competitors to release more formal frameworks for governing long-running agentic workloads. The governance gap here is significant and growing.
