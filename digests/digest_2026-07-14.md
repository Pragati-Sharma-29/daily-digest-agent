## Enterprise Security & Cyber Threats

### What happened
Today's security news was dominated by state-actor threats operating at unprecedented scale and sophistication. Russian hackers are targeting home routers for proxy networks, Iran demonstrated the lethal real-world consequences of telecom infrastructure vulnerabilities, and Google Cloud's public sector threat report paints a picture of adversaries now operating at machine speed. On the defensive side, a novel "context bombing" technique is showing promise against AI-powered attack agents.

### Key stories
- **Russia state hackers targeting routers** — CISA issued an urgent warning that Russian state-sponsored actors are actively compromising consumer and small business routers to build residential proxy networks, which are increasingly used to launder malicious traffic and evade detection. The advisory urges router users to apply firmware updates and change default credentials, though the practical reach of such warnings to non-technical users remains limited. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/07/the-us-government-warns-that-russia-state-hackers-are-coming-after-your-router/))
- **Iran used SS7 vulnerabilities to locate US military** — A report revealed that the Iranian government exploited well-known SS7 signaling protocol flaws in mobile networks to physically locate US military personnel in the Middle East, with lethal consequences in the lead-up to and beginning of the conflict. This represents one of the most consequential real-world weaponizations of telecom infrastructure vulnerabilities ever documented and reinforces long-standing calls to modernize SS7. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/14/iran-abused-mobile-networks-vulnerabilities-to-locate-u-s-military-in-the-middle-east-report-says/))
- **Defenders embrace "context bombing" against AI hacking agents** — Ars Technica reports on a technique where defenders intentionally flood the context windows of AI-powered hacking agents with irrelevant or contradictory information, causing the agents to shut down before completing attacks. This is a fascinating arms-race development — the same prompt injection vulnerabilities that make AI agents dangerous to defenders can be weaponized against offensive AI systems. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/07/now-defenders-are-embracing-the-prompt-injection-too/))
- **2026 Public Sector M-Trends Report** — Google Cloud's Mandiant released key findings showing that public sector defenders are no longer protecting a traditional perimeter but a complex web of interconnected trust relationships, with adversaries now operating at machine speed. The report signals that AI-accelerated attacks are becoming the norm in government-targeted intrusions. ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/public-sector/key-findings-from-the-2026-public-sector-m-trends-report-and-beyond/))

### What to watch
The SS7 vulnerability story will likely prompt congressional pressure for telecom infrastructure modernization. Watch for whether CISA issues more specific guidance on router security, and monitor how the "context bombing" defensive technique evolves as offensive AI agents become more resilient to context manipulation.

## Quantum Computing

### What happened
PsiQuantum published the most detailed public engineering roadmap yet for a large-scale fault-tolerant quantum computer, built on photonic rather than superconducting or ion-trap approaches. The scale and specificity of the announcement — 100 stainless steel cabinets in a data-center-like facility — distinguishes it from previous quantum computing announcements that have often been criticized for vagueness.

### Key stories
- **PsiQuantum's plan for a massive photonic quantum computer** — MIT Tech Review reports that PsiQuantum's machine will be housed in a room resembling a data center crossed with an ice cream factory (due to cryogenic requirements), with roughly 100 six-foot cabinets. The photonic approach is notable because light-based qubits have inherent advantages in error correction and scalability, but the company has faced skepticism about timelines — this level of architectural detail is an attempt to address that credibility gap. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/14/1140356/psiquantum-plan-massive-quantum-computer-out-of-light/))

### What to watch
Watch for peer review of PsiQuantum's engineering claims and any announcements about government or enterprise partnerships to fund the build-out. Google, IBM, and Microsoft's responses to this roadmap — either as competitive positioning or partnership interest — will be telling.
