## AI Bias & Hiring

### What happened
A new study highlighted by MIT Technology Review delivers an uncomfortable finding: AI systems used for hiring are more prone to forming biases than human reviewers — not less. As AI-powered resume screening becomes increasingly standard practice across enterprises, this research challenges one of the core justifications for automating hiring (removing human bias) and has significant legal and regulatory implications.

### Key stories
- **AI is more likely than humans to form biases when hiring** — Researchers found that large language models pick up and amplify biases from training data when screening job applicants, performing worse than human reviewers in terms of fairness across demographic groups. The finding is alarming given how rapidly AI resume screening has been adopted, often on the implicit assumption that machines are more objective than people. Regulatory scrutiny of algorithmic hiring tools seems likely to intensify significantly as a result. ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/20/1140655/ai-biases-hiring-humans/))
- **The Download: AI hiring biases and weather data sabotage** — MIT Tech Review's daily briefing packaged the hiring bias story alongside coverage of weather data infrastructure being deliberately sabotaged, a reminder that the integrity of data pipelines — whether for AI training or climate modeling — is increasingly a national security concern. The pairing of these two stories underscores a broader theme: data quality and integrity are foundational to responsible AI deployment. ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/20/1140664/the-download-ai-hiring-biases-weather-data-sabotage/))

### What to watch
Look for regulatory bodies like the EEOC in the U.S. and equivalent bodies in the EU to cite this research as justification for new rules around algorithmic hiring transparency. Companies already using AI for screening should be re-auditing their tools immediately.

## Cybersecurity

### What happened
A pair of serious cybersecurity incidents dominated the day. WordPress sites are under active attack from hackers exploiting two recently-patched critical vulnerabilities, and healthcare tech firm Craneware confirmed that a "significant" amount of customer data was stolen in a breach — data belonging to the thousands of U.S. hospitals, pharmacies, and clinics that rely on the company for billing software. The healthcare breach in particular echoes the 2024 Change Healthcare attack in scope and systemic risk.

### Key stories
- **Hackers exploiting critical WordPress vulnerabilities** — Two critical security flaws in WordPress software are being actively exploited, potentially giving attackers remote control over tens of millions of websites. The bugs were recently patched, meaning the attack window is users who haven't yet updated — a chronic problem given WordPress's fragmented update ecosystem across self-hosted sites and third-party managed installations. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/hackers-are-exploiting-recently-patched-wordpress-bugs-putting-millions-of-websites-at-risk/))
- **Craneware healthcare data breach** — Edinburgh-based Craneware, which provides billing and financial management software to thousands of U.S. hospitals, pharmacies, and clinics, confirmed that hackers stole a "significant" amount of customer data. The implications are severe: billing software for healthcare providers contains some of the most sensitive patient and financial data in existence, and breaches at this layer of the healthcare supply chain can cascade to affect millions of patients. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/hackers-stole-significant-amount-of-data-from-tech-firm-relied-on-by-thousands-of-us-hospitals-and-pharmacies/))
- **Hacker wipes Romania's entire land registry database** — A destructive cyberattack wiped Romania's national land registry database — a potentially catastrophic loss of property ownership records that could affect millions of citizens and property transactions. This type of destructive (rather than exfiltration-focused) attack represents a particularly dangerous threat vector for critical national infrastructure. ([Hacker News / Risky Biz](https://news.risky.biz/risky-bulletin-hacker-wipes-romanias-entire-land-registry-database/))
- **SEBI fines CDSL ₹1 crore over 2022 malware attack** — India's markets regulator SEBI penalized the Central Depository Services Limited for cybersecurity failures that enabled a 2022 malware attack, disrupting depository operations and market settlements. The penalty signals that financial regulators globally are increasingly willing to hold financial infrastructure operators directly accountable for security failures. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/stocks/news/sebi-imposes-rs-1-crore-penalty-on-cdsl-over-2022-malware-attack/articleshow/132518202.cms))

### What to watch
The Craneware breach warrants close follow-up — expect HHS and state regulators to investigate whether HIPAA obligations were triggered, and watch for class-action litigation from affected healthcare providers. On WordPress, the key question is how quickly the WordPress security community can drive patch adoption at scale across millions of self-hosted sites.

## AI & Scientific Data Preservation

### What happened
With U.S. federal funding for science under pressure, researchers are taking matters into their own hands — literally copying massive NASA climate and environmental datasets onto European supercomputers for safekeeping and AI training. This story sits at the intersection of AI data needs, geopolitical data sovereignty, and the fragility of publicly-funded scientific infrastructure.

### Key stories
- **NASA climate data migrated to Swiss supercomputer amid U.S. funding cuts** — Researchers have transferred large quantities of publicly-available NASA climate and environmental data to a Swiss supercomputer, both to protect it from potential loss under U.S. funding rollbacks and to use it for training AI climate models. The move reflects a growing international effort to ensure that scientific data commons built with public money aren't lost to political shifts — a form of scientific data hedging that has profound implications for AI model development globally. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/nasa-climate-data-copied-to-swiss-supercomputer-for-ai-training-amid-us-funding-cuts/articleshow/132517258.cms))

### What to watch
Watch for whether other major U.S. scientific data repositories — NOAA, NIH, USGS — face similar preservation initiatives from international research partners. The broader pattern of scientific data being "rescued" to European infrastructure is a significant geopolitical shift in where AI training data for climate and health applications will reside.
