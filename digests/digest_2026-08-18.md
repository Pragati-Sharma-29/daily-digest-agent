## AI Safety, Surveillance & Ethics

### What happened
Two major ethics stories dominated today. MIT Tech Review published a sharp critique of Flock Safety's license plate camera network and how its defenders miss fundamental design-choice concerns. Separately, MIT Tech Review also raised the broader issue that AI usage data from companies like Anthropic and OpenAI is systematically curated before release, making it impossible for independent researchers to understand how AI is actually being used.

### Key stories
- **What Flock's defenders are missing** — MIT Tech Review's analysis argues that debates around Flock Safety's 120,000+ camera network focus on outcomes (crime reduction) while ignoring intentional design choices that affect civil liberties — such as who gets access to the data and under what legal standards. This is a critical framework for evaluating AI-powered surveillance tools: it's not just whether they work, but how they're architected to constrain or enable misuse. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/17/1142200/what-flocks-defenders-are-missing/))
- **We still don't know how people are really using AI** — MIT Tech Review reports that AI companies' published usage reports reflect curated, self-selected data, making it impossible for independent researchers to assess real usage patterns, harms, or benefits. This opacity is a systemic problem: policy, regulation, and public understanding are all being built on data controlled entirely by the companies being regulated. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/18/1142226/how-people-use-ai/))
- **AI's recursive self-improvement might not come so quickly after all** — MIT Tech Review pushes back on the industry's boldest claim — that AI will soon improve itself autonomously with minimal human oversight. While LLMs can write code and generate synthetic training data, the article argues the feedback loops needed for truly recursive self-improvement face significant technical and evaluation challenges that are routinely understated. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/18/1142188/ai-recursive-self-improvement/))

### What to watch
The AI usage data opacity issue is ripe for legislative intervention — watch for Congressional or EU proposals requiring standardized, third-party-auditable AI usage reporting. The Flock debate will intensify as more municipalities adopt similar camera networks.

## Cybersecurity & AI Security

### What happened
Two significant security stories broke today — one involving a major AI product vulnerability at Microsoft, and another involving Google Cloud's proactive use of agentic AI for defensive security. Together they illustrate both the new attack surfaces that AI products create and the AI-powered defenses being developed to counter them.

### Key stories
- **Microsoft Copilot reveals secret input that allowed it to be hacked** — Researchers discovered a hidden parameter in Microsoft Copilot that allowed attackers to steal user passwords by simply getting a target to click a link. The vulnerability is particularly alarming given Copilot's deep integration with Microsoft 365 enterprise environments — a single exploit in an AI assistant can cascade into full credential compromise across an organization. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/08/microsoft-copilot-reveals-secret-input-that-allowed-it-to-be-hacked/))
- **Staying ahead of adversarial AI through agentic source code review** — Google Cloud published a detailed approach to using agentic AI to detect adversarial AI intrusions by automatically reviewing source code for signs of data exfiltration or extortion setups. The approach represents a meaningful shift: using AI to defend against AI-accelerated attacks, a cat-and-mouse dynamic that will define enterprise security for the next decade. ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/staying-ahead-of-adversarial-ai-through-agentic-source-code-review/))
- **OpenAI's cybersecurity posture: The Defender's Window** — OpenAI's security report argues that AI tools give defenders meaningful advantages right now — better threat detection, faster incident response, and more scalable security operations — but that the window of advantage is not permanent and requires security teams to move quickly. The report is part security guidance, part advocacy for AI adoption in enterprise security stacks. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/the-defenders-window))

### What to watch
The Microsoft Copilot vulnerability will accelerate scrutiny of other enterprise AI assistant integrations — watch for security researchers to probe Google Workspace AI, Salesforce Einstein, and similar products for analogous hidden parameters or injection attack surfaces.

TSMC's aggressive global fab construction program and its sophisticated strategy for extracting value from older process nodes were highlighted today. Meanwhile, India's semiconductor ambitions are being squeezed by macroeconomic pressures, and Anthro Energy broke ground on a solid-state battery materials factory — a different but related front in the hardware infrastructure race.

### Key stories
- **How TSMC uses old fabs to make new chips** — Asianometry's deep-dive explains TSMC's strategy of running simultaneous leading-edge fab builds (Fab 20 in Hsinchu, Fab 22 in Kaohsiung) while keeping older fabs productive for mature-node chips that are still critical for automotive, IoT, and legacy applications. This "full-stack" fab strategy is how TSMC maintains margin discipline and capacity flexibility that competitors struggle to replicate. ([Asianometry](https://asianometry.passport.online/member/episode/how-tsmc-uses-old-fabs-to-make-new-chips))
- **ISM outlay faces currency pressure** — India's Semiconductor Mission subsidies have been eroded by a weak rupee, reducing their effective dollar value at exactly the moment India needs to attract capital-intensive fab investments. This is a structural vulnerability in subsidy-based industrial policy when denominated in local currency — a lesson for other emerging semiconductor nations. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/newsletters/tech-top-5/ism-outlay-faces-currency-pressure-etsa-2026-best-on-campus-comeback-kid-nominees/articleshow/133323467.cms))
- **Anthro Energy breaks ground on solid-state battery factory** — Battery materials startup Anthro Energy broke ground on a Louisville, Kentucky factory to manufacture electrolytes for next-generation batteries, including solid-state cells. Solid-state batteries remain the holy grail for EVs and energy storage, and domestic U.S. production of key materials addresses a critical supply chain dependency on Asian manufacturers. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/18/anthro-energy-breaks-ground-on-factory-that-could-pave-the-road-to-solid-state-batteries/))

### What to watch
Watch TSMC's Arizona fab progress and whether the multi-geography build strategy creates meaningful geopolitical risk diversification or merely symbolic diversification while Taiwan remains the irreplaceable core.
