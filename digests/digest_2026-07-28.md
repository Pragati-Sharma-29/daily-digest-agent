## AI Infrastructure & Power Crisis

### What happened
The collision between AI's insatiable power demands and real-world grid capacity reached a new inflection point today, with PJM — the largest US electricity grid — reportedly considering temporary power cuts to data centers to prevent broader blackouts. This is arguably the most significant near-term constraint on AI scaling that doesn't involve chips or capital.

### Key stories
- **PJM grid may cut data center power temporarily to prevent blackouts** — PJM, which manages electricity for 65 million people across 13 states, is considering temporary load-shedding for data centers as AI construction outpaces grid expansion. This is a genuinely alarming development: it means AI infrastructure growth has already exceeded the pace at which utilities can safely accommodate it, and that major hyperscalers may face operational disruptions. The long-term fix — new generation capacity and grid upgrades — takes years, not months. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/data-centers-may-face-temporary-power-cuts-to-prevent-blackouts-on-largest-us-grid/))
- **Best Buy secures AI workloads on Google Cloud with Workforce Identity Federation** — Best Buy's case study with Google Cloud illustrates a quieter dimension of AI infrastructure: securing identity and access management as AI workloads proliferate. Best Buy used Workforce Identity Federation to manage access across its expanding AI stack, reducing administrative friction while maintaining security posture. This is increasingly a standard enterprise challenge as AI deployments scale. ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/retail/best-buy-scales-secure-ai-access-with-workforce-identity-federation/))
- **Kimi Linear: A new attention architecture from Moonshot AI** — A paper on Kimi Linear appeared on Hacker News, presenting an "expressive, efficient attention architecture" that could offer improvements in the compute/performance tradeoff for large language models. Architectural innovations like this are increasingly important as the industry looks for ways to do more with less compute — especially relevant given the power constraints described above. ([arXiv via Hacker News](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26692))

### What to watch
Track PJM's formal policy decisions on data center load-shedding — if adopted, this could set a precedent that spreads to other grid operators across the US and Europe. Also monitor whether hyperscalers accelerate investments in on-site power generation (nuclear, natural gas) as a result.

## Semiconductors & Memory Technology

### What happened
Semiconductor news today spanned a talent crisis at Samsung, a technical deep-dive into the future of DRAM architecture, and the ongoing geopolitical squeeze on chip equipment makers. Together these stories paint a picture of an industry under simultaneous technological, organizational, and political pressure.

### Key stories
- **Samsung chip engineers are fleeing to SK Hynix** — MIT Tech Review reported on a growing morale and talent crisis at Samsung's semiconductor division, with engineers disengaging and defecting to rival SK Hynix. Samsung has struggled to keep pace with SK Hynix in HBM (High Bandwidth Memory) for AI applications, and this talent exodus could compound its competitive difficulties. For the AI supply chain, Samsung's struggles matter because it is one of the few companies capable of producing cutting-edge memory at scale. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/28/1140853/samsung-chip-workers-exodus-sk-hynix/))
- **Asianometry: True 3D DRAM is the next frontier in memory density** — Asianometry's latest deep-dive covers "True 3D DRAM" — the approach of stacking DRAM cells vertically, analogous to what 3D NAND did for flash storage. As lateral scaling hits physical limits, 3D DRAM is increasingly seen as the only path to the bit density gains that AI training and inference workloads demand. The manufacturing challenges are formidable, but the competitive stakes — particularly for SK Hynix, Micron, and Samsung — are enormous. ([Asianometry](https://asianometry.passport.online/member/episode/true-3-d-dram))
- **ASML caught in the US-China chip equipment squeeze** — ASML faces a structural dilemma: US export controls are cutting off its access to China's large and growing semiconductor market, while China is actively investing to develop domestic alternatives to ASML's lithography equipment. This is a slow-moving but potentially existential threat to ASML's China business, which has historically been a major revenue contributor. The company has few good options in the near term. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/chinas-chip-tool-push-shows-asml-caught-in-us-china-squeeze/articleshow/132689198.cms))
- **4GB graphics cards return as component costs rise** — AsRock listed an AMD Radeon RX 9050 with only 4GB of VRAM, the first modern GPU to ship with such limited memory, as RAM price increases and component shortages push manufacturers toward cost-cutting. This is a regression that will meaningfully limit the card's usefulness for modern gaming and AI tasks, and signals that consumer hardware affordability is becoming a genuine concern again. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/games/971868/asrock-amd-4gb-rx-9050-gpu))

### What to watch
Watch Samsung's Q2/Q3 results and any announcements regarding HBM market share — if SK Hynix continues to dominate HBM supply for NVIDIA, Samsung's semiconductor division faces a strategic crisis, not just a morale one. Also track any Chinese announcements on domestic lithography progress as a barometer of how fast ASML's moat is eroding.
