## Agentic AI & Productivity

### What happened
Anthropic expanded Claude Cowork from a desktop-only product to full mobile and web availability for Max subscribers, enabling true cross-device task handoffs. Google Cloud simultaneously published a major enterprise guidance package — including a 20-question framework for agentic deployment and a developer guide to publishing agents in the Gemini Enterprise marketplace — reflecting how the cloud giants are racing to own the agentic infrastructure layer.

### Key stories
- **Claude Cowork expands to mobile and web** — Anthropic's collaborative AI agent environment, previously limited to laptops, now allows users to start a task at their desk, check status on mobile, and continue on the web. This is a direct play for the "always-on AI colleague" use case and puts pressure on competitors like Microsoft Copilot and Google's Workspace AI to match cross-device continuity. The expansion signals that the coding agent wars are indeed spilling into general office workflows. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/07/the-coding-agent-wars-are-spilling-into-the-rest-of-the-office-claude-cowork/))
- **Google Cloud: 83% of organizations need infrastructure upgrades for agentic AI** — A new Google Cloud report finds that the vast majority of enterprises are not yet equipped to run agentic AI workloads, citing compute, networking, and orchestration gaps. This is simultaneously a market research finding and a sales document, but the underlying tension is real: enterprises rushed to deploy LLM-based chatbots on existing infrastructure and are now hitting walls. ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/compute/state-of-ai-infrastructure-report-overview/))
- **Google Cloud publishes developer guide for Agents-as-a-Service** — Google's guide to publishing agents in the Gemini Enterprise marketplace frames "AaaS" (Agents-as-a-Service) as the successor to SaaS, using standardized open protocols for agent interoperability. This matters because it represents a concrete attempt to define the distribution and monetization layer for AI agents — whoever owns the marketplace owns the ecosystem. ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/developers-practitioners/publish-agents-in-gemini-enterprise-and-google-cloud-marketplace/))

### What to watch
Watch how quickly Microsoft responds to Claude Cowork's mobile/web expansion with enhancements to Copilot's cross-device experience. The agent marketplace competition between Google (Gemini Enterprise), Microsoft (Copilot Studio), and Anthropic will define enterprise AI distribution for the next several years.

## AI Security & Cybercrime

### What happened
Two major AI security stories collided today: the confirmation of the first AI-executed ransomware attack in the wild, and the launch of a consumer app designed to protect individuals from increasingly convincing AI-generated scams. Enterprise security firm Root.io (just acquired by Aikido) also argued that AI-driven vulnerability management is no longer optional given the scale of the backlog.

### Key stories
- **The 'first' AI-run ransomware attack still needed a human** — An AI agent autonomously executed the technical components of a real-world ransomware attack — the first confirmed case — but a human operator still selected the victim, built the infrastructure, and provided credentials. This distinction matters enormously for legal liability and threat modeling, but it doesn't diminish the milestone: the barrier to executing sophisticated cyberattacks is falling as AI handles the hard technical work. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/06/the-first-ai-run-ransomware-attack-still-needed-a-human/))
- **Savi raises $7M to protect consumers from AI scams** — Savi's newly launched iPhone and Android app targets a growing threat: hyper-realistic AI-generated scam calls, including fake kidnapping ransom demands using cloned voices of family members. The $7M seed round reflects investor conviction that consumer AI safety is an underserved market as deepfake audio/video quality crosses the believability threshold for ordinary people. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/07/savis-app-aims-to-protect-consumers-from-realistic-ai-scams-like-kidnappers-demanding-ransom/))
- **Root.io acquired by Aikido: AI as the only fix for the security backlog** — Insight Partners-backed Root.io, now acquired by Aikido, built the case that enterprise vulnerability management cannot scale with human headcount alone — AI triage and remediation is the only sustainable model. The acquisition consolidates two AI-native security players and signals that the security tooling market is entering a consolidation phase. ([Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/ideas/root-security-backlog-ai/))
- **Google Cloud + Wiz ASM: Proactive security with threat intelligence** — Google Cloud announced integration between Google Threat Intelligence and Wiz's Attack Surface Management product, enabling organizations to get personalized, AI-accelerated risk prioritization. As AI speeds up both the discovery and exploitation of vulnerabilities, the window between disclosure and exploitation is shrinking. ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/drive-proactive-security-prioritize-risks-with-google-threat-intelligence-and-wiz-asm/))

### What to watch
The AI ransomware story will prompt regulatory and law enforcement responses — watch for guidance from CISA and international cyber agencies on AI-assisted attack attribution. On the consumer side, watch whether Apple and Google move to build native deepfake call detection into iOS and Android, which would commoditize Savi's core feature.

Sam Altman's proposal to give every American family a ~$300 stake in OpenAI drew scrutiny from MIT Technology Review, which framed it as a politically savvy but structurally murky idea. Separately, the U.S. Treasury issued an AI warning related to systemic financial risk, and Ukraine announced a sovereign AI policy to run government AI systems exclusively on domestically controlled servers.

### Key stories

MIT Tech Review examines whether this is a genuine wealth-sharing mechanism or primarily a PR move to smooth the regulatory path for OpenAI's restructuring — the answer matters enormously for how AI profits get distributed in the future. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/06/1140176/your-familys-300-stake-in-openai/))
- **U.S. Treasury issues AI warning** — The Treasury Department flagged AI-related systemic risks to the financial system, adding an official government voice to concerns that have previously been raised primarily by academics and advocacy groups. This is an early signal that AI governance in financial services is moving from voluntary guidelines toward potential regulatory mandates. ([MIT Tech Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/07/1140197/the-download-your-openai-stake-treasury-ai-warning/))
- **Ukraine to require government AI models run without external provider control** — Ukraine's government announced it will favor AI systems hosted on its own servers for public services, explicitly to avoid dependence on foreign providers who could restrict access. This policy was reinforced after a reported incident of external AI service disruption, making it one of the most concrete examples of "AI sovereignty" policy being implemented in wartime conditions. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/ukraine-to-pick-ai-models-operated-without-provider-control-official-says/articleshow/132242843.cms))

### What to watch
The OpenAI equity proposal will face legal and regulatory scrutiny as the for-profit conversion proceeds — watch for SEC guidance and Congressional commentary. Ukraine's AI sovereignty stance will likely influence other governments' procurement policies, especially in Europe.
