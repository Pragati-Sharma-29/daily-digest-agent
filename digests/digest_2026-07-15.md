Two major signals emerged: Anthropic and Blackstone backed a new firm called Ode to embed forward-deployed engineers inside enterprises, and Insight Partners published a detailed thesis arguing that AI has fundamentally inverted the traditional enterprise software buying pattern.

### Key stories
- **Anthropic, Blackstone bet the next trillion-dollar AI business is implementation, not just models** — Ode launches with backing from Anthropic and Blackstone, premised on the idea that embedding specialized engineers inside enterprises to run AI workflows is the critical missing layer between frontier models and real-world ROI. This mirrors the "forward-deployed engineer" model pioneered by Palantir and suggests the AI industry is maturing past pure model competition into a professional services + software hybrid era. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/15/anthropic-blackstone-bet-the-next-trillion-dollar-ai-business-is-implementation-not-models/))

Forward-deployed engineers exist at the intersection of product, engineering, and customer success, and Insight argues this role will become central to every major enterprise AI vendor's go-to-market. ([Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/ideas/demystifying-forward-deployed-engineers/))
- **How to manage AI investments in the agentic era** — OpenAI published guidance for enterprises on how to measure AI ROI in an agentic context, specifically advocating for "useful work per dollar" as the key metric rather than traditional software KPIs. This signals that OpenAI is actively trying to help enterprise customers justify and scale their AI budgets, a critical move as competition for enterprise contracts intensifies. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/managing-ai-investments-in-agentic-era))
- **Madrona invests in Thira** — Madrona announced investment in Thira, a new company co-founded by Sunny Gupta and Kurt Shintaffer (the team behind Apptio), framing it as "the biggest opportunity yet." While details are sparse, the pedigree of the founders — who previously built and sold a major enterprise software company — suggests Thira is targeting a significant enterprise AI workflow problem. ([Madrona](https://www.madrona.com/why-we-invested-in-thira-partnering-again-with-sunny-gupta-and-kurt-shintaffer-on-the-biggest-opportunity-yet/))

### What to watch
Watch whether the "forward-deployed engineer" model becomes a standard GTM motion across AI labs, or whether it proves too expensive to scale — and whether Ode's early enterprise wins validate Anthropic and Blackstone's thesis that implementation is where the real trillion-dollar value is captured.

## OpenAI's Super App Strategy

### What happened
OpenAI made a significant strategic move by merging Codex into ChatGPT under the "ChatGPT Work" brand, publishing detailed workflow playbooks for sales and data science teams. Stratechery's Ben Thompson analyzed this as a potential abandonment of the chat category OpenAI pioneered, in favor of a broader agentic "super app" strategy.

### Key stories
- **The OpenAI Super App: ChatGPT = Codex, Whither Chat** — Ben Thompson's analysis argues that by folding Codex into ChatGPT and repositioning the product as a work execution platform, OpenAI is making a massive strategic bet that the future of AI interfaces is task completion rather than conversation. The piece raises the uncomfortable question of whether OpenAI is ceding the conversational AI space to competitors while betting everything on agentic enterprise workflows. ([Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/?p=19506))
- **How data science teams use ChatGPT Work** — OpenAI published a detailed playbook showing data science teams how to use ChatGPT Work to build root-cause briefs, KPI memos, and dashboard specs from real inputs — essentially positioning the product as an AI analyst that can own entire analytical workflows end-to-end, not just answer questions. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/academy/codex-for-work/how-data-science-teams-use-codex))
- **How sales teams use ChatGPT Work** — OpenAI's sales-focused playbook covers pipeline briefs, meeting prep, forecast reviews, account plans, and stalled-deal diagnoses — a direct assault on CRM-adjacent workflow tools like Salesforce Einstein and Clay. The depth and specificity of these use cases suggests OpenAI has moved well beyond experimentation into deliberate enterprise vertical penetration. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/academy/codex-for-work/how-sales-teams-use-codex))

### What to watch
The critical open question is whether collapsing the Codex and ChatGPT brands creates coherence or confusion for enterprise buyers — and whether pure-play conversational AI competitors (Anthropic's Claude, Google's Gemini) will capture users who want a simpler, non-agentic interface.

## Cybersecurity: Critical Infrastructure Threats

### What happened
Two major cybersecurity stories today illustrate the scale of systemic vulnerability in widely-deployed infrastructure. Microsoft's Secure Boot was found to have been silently broken for nearly a decade, while CISA issued urgent warnings about Russian state actors targeting consumer and enterprise routers to build proxy networks.

### Key stories
- **Microsoft's Secure Boot has been broken for a decade and no one noticed until now** — Security researchers found that Microsoft failed to revoke old legacy "shims" — bootloader components — meaning Secure Boot, a foundational security feature on virtually all modern Windows PCs, has been bypassable for approximately ten years. The implications are severe: any attacker with physical or deep software access could have bypassed boot-level security on affected systems without detection. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/07/microsoft-secure-boot-has-been-broken-for-most-of-its-existence/))
- **The US government warns that Russia state hackers are coming after your router** — CISA issued an advisory warning that Russian state-sponsored hackers are actively compromising consumer and enterprise routers to create residential proxy networks, which are used to obfuscate the origins of cyberattacks. The agency specifically urged router owners to apply firmware updates and change default credentials, as residential proxies are now a preferred tool for evading IP-based detection. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/07/the-us-government-warns-that-russia-state-hackers-are-coming-after-your-router/))
- **US charges Russian 'bulletproof' web hosts over $62M cybercrime operation** — A previously sealed 2024 indictment was unsealed, accusing three Russian nationals and two web-hosting companies of operating "bulletproof" hosting services that shielded hackers and enabled a cybercrime operation netting $62M from victims. The unsealing signals a new phase of US enforcement action against Russian cyber infrastructure. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/15/us-charges-russian-bulletproof-web-hosts-over-cyberattacks-that-netted-62m-from-cybercrime-victims/))
- **Securing the AI supply chain on GKE: k8s-aibom** — Google Cloud introduced k8s-aibom, a tool for generating automated AI Bills of Materials (AI BOMs) for AI workloads running on Google Kubernetes Engine. This addresses the growing "shadow AI" problem where developers deploy AI workloads without formal security registration, creating blind spots in enterprise security posture. ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/introducing-k8s-aibom-on-gke-for-automated-ai-bills-of-materials/))

### What to watch
The Microsoft Secure Boot story will require a major patching response across enterprise Windows fleets — watch for Microsoft's official disclosure and patch timeline, and whether exploits targeting the vulnerable shims emerge in the wild before patching is widespread.

## Quantum Computing

### What happened
PsiQuantum made one of its most detailed public disclosures yet, revealing its architectural approach to building a massive photonic quantum computer that could represent a credible path to fault-tolerant, utility-scale quantum computing.

### Key stories
- **PsiQuantum has a plan to make a massive quantum computer out of light** — PsiQuantum revealed that its machine will be housed in approximately 100 stainless-steel cabinets resembling a data center, using photonic qubits — quantum bits encoded in light — which are less susceptible to thermal noise than superconducting qubit approaches used by IBM and Google. The disclosure is notable for its engineering specificity, suggesting PsiQuantum is much further along in hardware design than previously understood. ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/14/1140356/psiquantum-plan-massive-quantum-computer-out-of-light/))
- **What Anthropic's latest AI discovery does — and doesn't — show** — MIT Tech Review's analysis of Anthropic's recent interpretability research cautions against over-reading the results, noting that while Anthropic's work on Claude's internal representations is scientifically significant, it does not yet provide the level of mechanistic understanding needed to reliably predict or control model behavior. This is an important corrective to hype around AI "interpretability" as a safety solution. ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/13/1140343/what-anthropics-latest-ai-discovery-does-and-doesnt-show/))

### What to watch
Watch for PsiQuantum's timeline announcements and whether its photonic approach achieves error-correction milestones that would put it ahead of superconducting competitors — the first team to demonstrate fault-tolerant logical qubits at scale will likely dominate the quantum computing market for a decade.
