## Privacy, Policy & Legal

### What happened
Today brought two major privacy developments in the U.S.: a landmark Supreme Court ruling limiting geofence warrants and a new legislative proposal targeting AI companies' use of sensitive personal health and location data. Together they represent a meaningful tightening of the legal environment around digital surveillance and AI data practices.

### Key stories
- **Supreme Court Rules Geofence Warrants Are Protected by Fourth Amendment** — The Supreme Court held that geofence warrants — which compel companies like Google to hand over location data for all devices in a geographic area — require Fourth Amendment protections. While privacy advocates celebrated the ruling, many sought an outright ban; the court's decision instead requires warrants to meet a higher constitutional standard, leaving some room for law enforcement use. This is one of the most significant digital privacy rulings in years. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/29/in-major-privacy-win-supreme-court-rules-geofence-warrants-are-protected-by-privacy-rights/))
- **Lawmakers Propose Ban on AI Companies Selling Health and Location Data** — Senators Warren and Scanlon introduced the Health and Location Data Protection Act, which would prohibit the sale of Americans' health and location information to data brokers — explicitly including data that users share with AI chatbots like ChatGPT or Claude. This is the first major legislative proposal to specifically address the data privacy risks of conversational AI systems, and it could fundamentally constrain how AI companies monetize user interactions. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/959033/health-location-data-protection-act-ai-warren-scanlon))
- **The Inevitable Weakness of Metrics in AI Governance** — MIT Technology Review's Download newsletter flagged a growing debate about the reliability of AI evaluation metrics — arguing that as AI systems optimize for benchmarks, the benchmarks themselves lose validity. This has direct implications for how regulators and enterprises assess AI safety and reliability claims. ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/06/29/1139834/the-download-metric-weaknesses-ai-elephant-warnings/))

### What to watch
The Health and Location Data Protection Act's progress in the Senate will be a bellwether for whether Congress is ready to impose real constraints on AI data practices ahead of the 2026 midterms. The Supreme Court's geofence decision will also face immediate challenges from law enforcement agencies seeking to define the new limits of compliant warrants.
