## Agentic AI

### What happened
The agent economy saw major investment and product moves today. OpenAI published new research quantifying how agents are transforming work by enabling longer and more complex task completion, while the market moved to build both the training infrastructure and evaluation layers that agents need to operate reliably.

### Key stories
- **OpenAI research: How agents are transforming work** — OpenAI released a paper showing AI agents are enabling workers to take on longer, more complex tasks across a variety of roles, not just technical ones. The research signals that OpenAI sees agentic productivity as a core part of its commercial narrative — expect this framing to drive enterprise sales conversations. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/how-agents-are-transforming-work))
- **Patronus AI raises $50M to stress-test AI agents** — The former Meta AI researchers behind Patronus are building "digital worlds" — simulated environments where AI agents can be tested against edge cases and failure modes before deployment. With demand described as "nearly insatiable," this points to a growing enterprise anxiety about deploying agents that haven't been rigorously evaluated. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/patronus-ai-lands-50m-to-build-digital-worlds-that-stress-test-ai-agents/))
- **General Intuition raises $320M to train agents on gameplay** — General Intuition is betting that millions of hours of video game action data can teach AI agents something closer to human intuition — the kind of reactive, embodied decision-making that text alone can't provide. At a $2.3B valuation, this is a major wager that simulation-based training is a viable path to more capable real-world agents. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/general-intuitions-2-3b-bet-that-video-games-can-train-ai-agents-for-the-real-world/))
- **Notion kills email app as users shift to AI agents** — Notion announced it is shutting down its Skiff-influenced email client, explicitly citing the fact that most users have migrated to AI agents to manage their inboxes. This is a striking real-world data point: agents aren't just supplementing human workflows, they're displacing purpose-built apps entirely. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/06/notion-killing-skiff-influenced-email-app-since-most-users-use-ai-agents-instead/))
- **Google DeepMind introduces computer use in Gemini 3.5 Flash** — Google DeepMind has added computer-use capabilities to Gemini 3.5 Flash, allowing the model to interact with desktop interfaces autonomously. This puts Google in direct competition with Anthropic's computer use feature and signals the rapid commoditization of agentic UI interaction. ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-computer-use-in-gemini-3-5-flash/))

### What to watch
The evaluation and testing layer for AI agents (Patronus, and others like it) is becoming critical infrastructure — watch for more investment here and for enterprises to make agent evaluation a procurement requirement. The race between Google and Anthropic on computer-use capabilities will also be worth tracking closely.

## Cybersecurity

### What happened
Two significant cybersecurity developments today: a coordinated global law enforcement operation took down infrastructure supporting a cybercrime "assembly line," while Google's threat intelligence team disclosed a new Russian state-sponsored backdoor tied to the long-running Turla APT group.

### Key stories
- **Operation Endgame disrupts cybercrime assembly line** — A coordinated international law enforcement operation simultaneously struck two widely used cybercrime tools in what authorities described as a "one-two punch." The framing of cybercrime as an "assembly line" — modular, scalable, and industrialized — underscores how professional the criminal ecosystem has become, and why single-tool takedowns have limited long-term impact without sustained operational pressure. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/06/one-two-punch-delivered-in-global-operation-disrupts-cybercrime-assembly-line/))
- **Google discloses STOCKSTAY: Turla's new .NET backdoor** — Google Threat Intelligence Group published a deep analysis of STOCKSTAY, a continuously developed .NET backdoor attributed to Turla, a Russian intelligence-linked APT. The "continually developed" nature of the malware signals that state-sponsored actors are investing heavily in maintaining persistent, evolving access to targets — making detection and remediation an ongoing arms race. ([Google Cloud Blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/stockstay-turla-intelligence-gathering/))
- **CVE-2026-LGTM incident report surfaces on Hacker News** — A detailed incident report tagged as CVE-2026-LGTM gained significant traction on Hacker News, though details remain sparse. The ironic naming (LGTM = "Looks Good To Me," a code review approval) suggests a vulnerability introduced through an insufficiently reviewed code change — a recurring theme in software supply chain security. ([Hacker News / nesbitt.io](https://nesbitt.io/2026/06/26/incident-report-cve-2026-lgtm.html))

### What to watch
Follow Operation Endgame's long-term impact — past operations have had limited staying power as criminal groups reconstitute. The STOCKSTAY disclosure is also a reminder to watch for Turla-linked activity targeting European government and defense networks.

## Climate Tech & Infrastructure

### What happened
Europe's record heat wave is creating cascading infrastructure failures — power plants shutting down, grids under stress — while scientists are beginning to document the neurological toll that extreme heat takes on human cognition. These stories together paint a picture of climate change as an immediate technology and infrastructure challenge, not a future one.

### Key stories
- **Europe's heat wave is shutting down power plants** — Extreme temperatures across Western Europe are forcing power plant shutdowns at a moment of peak electricity demand, creating grid stress that is a preview of what increasingly frequent heat events will do to energy infrastructure. The paradox — that hotter weather requires more cooling energy while simultaneously reducing generation capacity — is a core challenge for grid operators and climate tech investors. ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/06/25/1139690/europe-heat-wave-grid/))
- **Heat waves are measurably harming brain function** — New research highlighted by MIT Tech Review finds that extreme heat impairs cognitive performance in ways scientists are still working to fully characterize — affecting memory, decision-making, and reaction time. For a tech industry that is increasingly concerned with human-AI collaboration, the idea that the workforce's cognitive baseline is being degraded by climate events is a material risk. ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/06/26/1139760/heat-waves-mess-with-your-brain-scientists-are-trying-to-figure-out-why/))
- **Ukraine plans domestic AI computing capacity with Kyivstar** — Ukraine is partnering with telecom operator Kyivstar, backed by VEON, to build domestic AI computing infrastructure. Building sovereign AI capacity during an active war is a striking signal of how strategically important AI compute has become — this is infrastructure resilience planning under extreme conditions. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/ukraine-plans-domestic-ai-computing-capacity-with-kyivstar/articleshow/132018357.cms))

### What to watch
Watch how European grid operators respond to this heat wave with infrastructure investments — energy storage, demand response, and resilient generation are all likely to see renewed policy urgency. Ukraine's AI compute initiative is also worth tracking as a model for other countries seeking AI sovereignty.

## Google Research & Cloud

### What happened
Google published two notable research posts today — one on improving LLM recall through reasoning, and another on cloud cost optimization — while Google Cloud's infrastructure team announced cross-region backup improvements.

### Key stories
- **Google Research: Reasoning unlocks parametric knowledge in LLMs** — A new Google Research paper demonstrates that prompting LLMs to reason before retrieving facts significantly improves their ability to surface knowledge they already "know" but fail to recall without structured thinking. ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/thinking-to-recall-how-reasoning-unlocks-parametric-knowledge-in-llms/))

As inference costs become a major budget line item for AI-heavy companies, techniques like this will matter increasingly. ([Google Research](https://research.google/blog/optimizing-cloud-economics-with-linear-elastic-caching/))

This is a practical infrastructure improvement that addresses a real gap in the cost/resilience tradeoff for enterprise customers. ([Google Cloud Blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/storage-data-transfer/backup-and-dr-service-adds-cross-region-backups/))

### What to watch
The "thinking to recall" research is worth following as it could influence how enterprise AI systems are architected — particularly for knowledge management and enterprise search use cases where LLMs are expected to surface precise factual information.
