## AI Chip Export Controls & Geopolitics

### What happened
The U.S. government's campaign to restrict AI chip flows to China is intensifying, with Nvidia pausing server shipments across Southeast Asia and the Netherlands actively lobbying against proposed U.S. legislation that would extend controls to semiconductor manufacturing equipment. These moves signal a broadening of the tech cold war beyond chips themselves to the full supply chain.

### Key stories
- **Nvidia pauses chip shipments to Southeast Asia** — Nvidia has instructed manufacturing partners to halt server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia, as the U.S. government seeks to close loopholes that allow Chinese entities to obtain restricted AI chips through third-country intermediaries. The move will have immediate commercial impact on data center buildouts across the region and signals that Southeast Asia's AI infrastructure ambitions are now collateral damage in the U.S.-China chip war. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/26/nvidia-hits-pause-on-chip-shipments-to-southeast-asia/))
- **Dutch PM to raise MATCH Act with Trump at G20** — The Netherlands is lobbying directly against the bipartisan MATCH Act, which would extend U.S. export controls to cover semiconductor lithography equipment — technology dominated by Dutch firm ASML. This diplomatic push underscores how U.S. chip export policy is straining alliances with key European partners who have deep economic stakes in semiconductor equipment exports to China. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/dutch-pm-to-discuss-us-match-act-chipmaking-export-controls-with-trump-at-g20/articleshow/136225240.cms))

### What to watch
The G20 conversation between the Dutch PM and Trump on the MATCH Act is a key inflection point — if the U.S. softens the bill's scope, it could provide ASML and similar firms a path forward; if not, European semiconductor equipment firms face a fundamental strategic threat to their China business.

## Cybersecurity: AI-Assisted Attacks & Critical Infrastructure

### What happened
This week marks a significant milestone in cybersecurity threat escalation: Switzerland has confirmed the first documented cases of AI agents being used to breach critical infrastructure, while a wave of attacks continues to hit U.S. government systems, healthcare firms, and consumer platforms. The sophistication and breadth of active threats is unusually high heading into the Black Friday/holiday period.

### Key stories
- **Switzerland confirms AI-assisted cyberattacks on critical infrastructure** — Switzerland's national cybersecurity agency confirmed that AI agents were involved in four of five recent breaches of critical infrastructure operators — the first confirmed cases of this attack vector in the country. This is a watershed moment: AI-assisted attacks are no longer theoretical, and the speed and adaptability advantages they give attackers will force a fundamental rethink of defensive postures across both government and private sectors. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/switzerland-confirms-ai-assisted.html))
- **US Air Force Cloud One platform reportedly breached** — Hackers reportedly breached the US Air Force's Cloud One computing platform, the latest in a string of attacks affecting federal agencies including the FBI, CIA, and Treasury Department. The pattern suggests a sustained, coordinated campaign against U.S. government cloud infrastructure, with implications for classified data security and the government's cloud-first migration strategy. ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/us-air-force-cloud-platform-reportedly-hacked-in-latest-federal-breach/))
- **pCloud password manager hacked despite 2FA** — pCloud's password manager suffered a breach in which attackers connected unknown devices to user accounts even when two-factor authentication was enabled, suggesting either a session token theft or authentication bypass attack. Password managers are high-value targets precisely because a successful breach gives attackers access to all downstream credentials — users of pCloud Pass should rotate master passwords and audit active sessions immediately. ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/pcloud-confirms-password-manager-hack-after-suspicious-login-alerts/))
- **Cacti zero-day with public PoC enables remote code execution** — An unpatched zero-day in the widely-used Cacti network monitoring platform has been publicly disclosed with a working proof-of-concept exploit, enabling unauthenticated remote code execution. The public availability of the PoC means active exploitation is likely imminent, and organizations running Cacti should take emergency mitigating action given the platform's typical deep network visibility. ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/unpatched-cacti-zero-day-with-public-poc-gives-remote-code-execution/))

### What to watch
The Switzerland AI-attack confirmation is the story to track: other national cybersecurity agencies are likely conducting similar assessments, and expect a wave of coordinated policy responses around AI-specific defensive requirements for critical infrastructure operators in the coming months.

## Black Friday Cybersecurity Threats

### What happened
Black Friday has arrived with a fresh set of consumer-facing cyber threats, most notably credit card skimming scripts exploiting WebRTC being discovered on popular shopping sites. The holiday shopping surge creates ideal conditions for these attacks as transaction volumes spike and consumer vigilance drops.

### Key stories
- **WebRTC-based credit card skimming on major shopping sites** — Researchers discovered credit card skimming scripts that exploit WebRTC — typically used for real-time browser communications — to exfiltrate payment card data from popular e-commerce sites. The technique is notable because WebRTC-based data exfiltration can bypass traditional network monitoring tools that focus on HTTP/HTTPS traffic, making these skimmers harder to detect. ([Infohawk via Hacker News](https://www.infohawk.com/news/2026-11-27-black-friday-webrtc-skimmer))
- **Convex AI skills sending user app ideas to remote servers without disclosure** — A GitHub issue revealed that Convex's AI development skills were transmitting users' application ideas to remote servers without adequately informing users, raising significant privacy and intellectual property concerns. As AI-assisted development tools proliferate, this episode highlights the gap between what users expect about data handling and what actually happens under the hood. ([GitHub via Hacker News](https://github.com/convex/convex-backend-skill/issues/64))
- **Boston Scientific data breach exposes personal and medical information** — Medical technology company Boston Scientific is notifying individuals affected by a cyberattack that exposed personal and medical data. Healthcare remains one of the most targeted sectors for data breaches given the sensitivity and long shelf-life of medical records for identity theft and fraud. ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-boston-scientific-data-breach-exposes-personal-medical-info/))

### What to watch
The WebRTC skimmer technique is worth monitoring closely — if it spreads across more shopping platforms before the holiday season peaks, it could result in one of the larger payment card theft events of the year.

## Software Supply Chain & State-Sponsored Hacking

### What happened
Two distinct threat vectors in the software supply chain emerged today: a trojanization attack on legitimate desktop software distributed through an official website, and a Vietnamese state-sponsored espionage campaign targeting Southeast Asian military and government organizations related to South China Sea territorial disputes.

### Key stories
- **Hackers trojanize Q-Dir and DesktopOK downloads on official site** — Attackers tampered with the installation files for Q-Dir (a file explorer) and DesktopOK distributed via the official SoftwareOK website, delivering remote access malware to users who downloaded what they believed were legitimate tools. Supply chain attacks via official distribution channels are particularly insidious because they undermine the basic trust users place in vendor websites — a problem that existing security tooling struggles to address at scale. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-trojanize-official-q-dir-and.html))
- **Vietnam-linked hackers target Southeast Asian military and government organizations** — A suspected Vietnamese state-sponsored hacking group has been attributed to espionage attacks against military, government, and legislative organizations across Southeast Asia, with a focus on intelligence related to South China Sea defense policy. The campaign illustrates how territorial disputes in the region are increasingly being fought in cyberspace alongside diplomatic and military channels. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/suspected-vietnam-linked-hackers-target.html))
- **Microsoft Store app delivers Vinci RAT for remote system control** — Researchers disclosed an attack in which a threat actor used a Microsoft Store application to distribute the Vinci remote access trojan, capable of full remote system control. The abuse of Microsoft's trusted app store distribution channel for malware delivery is a significant escalation that puts pressure on Microsoft to improve vetting processes for Store submissions. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/microsoft-store-app-found-delivering.html))

### What to watch
The trojanization of officially-distributed software is a growing attack vector — watch for industry and government response around mandatory code-signing verification and secure software distribution standards that go beyond what most vendors currently implement.
