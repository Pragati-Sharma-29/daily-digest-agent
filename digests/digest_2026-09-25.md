## Security, Privacy & Cryptography

### What happened
Today was an unusually bad day for the security landscape across multiple dimensions: a new RSA-breaking technique has been discovered, Supabase customers are leaking data en masse due to AI-generated app misconfigurations, Kiteworks is warning of an imminent cyberattack, and crypto exchange Bitget lost $350 million in a hack. The common thread is that AI is simultaneously expanding attack surfaces (through vibe-coded apps) and enabling new classes of attacks (against cryptographic foundations).

### Key stories
- **There's a new way to break RSA that's faster than anything we've seen before** — Researchers have published a new RSA-breaking method that doesn't rely on traditional integer factoring — the assumption that has underpinned RSA's security for decades. This is potentially a landmark cryptography result with significant implications for any system currently relying on RSA encryption, which includes much of the internet's security infrastructure. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/09/theres-a-new-way-to-break-rsa-thats-faster-than-anything-weve-seen-before/))
- **Some Supabase customers are publicly exposing reams of people's data to the web** — Developers using Supabase who built apps with AI assistance (vibe-coding) are misconfiguring their databases and publicly exposing user data at scale, with security researchers finding multiple instances. This is the clearest evidence yet that AI-assisted development lowers the skill floor for building apps without lowering the knowledge floor for securing them — a dangerous gap that will produce ongoing data exposure incidents. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/25/some-supabase-customers-are-publicly-exposing-reams-of-peoples-data-to-web/))
- **Kiteworks urges customers to shut down their servers amid 'imminent' threat of cyberattack** — Kiteworks, which enables companies to transfer large datasets over the internet, received a "credible threat" from law enforcement about an imminent attack and is urging customers to take their servers offline. The severity of the warning — asking paying customers to accept service disruption — suggests the threat is serious and potentially nation-state-level in sophistication. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/25/kiteworks-urges-customers-to-shut-down-their-servers-amid-imminent-threat-of-cyberattack/))
- **Crypto exchange Bitget pauses withdrawals after $350 million stolen in hack** — Bitget's CEO confirmed unauthorized transfers from wallets totaling approximately $350 million, prompting a temporary freeze on customer withdrawals. The scale of the theft makes it one of the larger crypto exchange hacks in recent years, and the fact that customer withdrawals were suspended means ordinary users are bearing the immediate consequences. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/crypto-exchange-bitget-pauses-withdrawals-after-350-million-stolen-in-hack/articleshow/134483030.cms))

### What to watch
The new RSA-breaking research deserves close follow-up from the cryptography community to assess its practical threat level and whether it accelerates the timeline for post-quantum cryptography migration. The Supabase data exposure story will also likely prompt Supabase to introduce more aggressive default security configurations for AI-generated code.

## Memory Technology & AI Hardware

### What happened

### Key stories

As LLMs get larger and KV caches grow, DRAM alone can't economically serve as the memory layer — High Bandwidth Flash offers a middle path between expensive HBM and slow commodity storage. ([Asianometry](https://asianometry.passport.online/member/episode/high-bandwidth-flash-is-coming))
- **SK Hynix's Solidigm weighs IPO that could value the unit at up to $150 billion** — Solidigm, SK Hynix's NAND flash subsidiary, held investment bank pitch meetings this week in a formal "bake-off" process, marking a concrete step toward an IPO. A $150 billion valuation would make it one of the largest semiconductor IPOs ever, reflecting investor appetite for any company positioned in the AI infrastructure supply chain. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/sk-hynixs-solidigm-weighs-ipo-that-could-value-the-unit-at-up-to-150-billion-report/articleshow/134489719.cms))
- **Tesla's Optimus robot is going through growing pains** — Tesla's humanoid robot program is falling significantly short of production targets, producing only "several hundred robots a week" against a stated goal of 20,000 per week, according to The Information. This illustrates the persistent gap between Elon Musk's production timelines and manufacturing reality, and raises questions about how quickly physical AI systems can actually scale compared to software-only AI. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1000794/tesla-optimus-production-issues-hands))

### What to watch
The Solidigm IPO process will be a key signal for how public markets are valuing AI infrastructure picks-and-shovels plays in 2026. Watch for the IPO timeline and whether HBM competitors like Micron position themselves in the High Bandwidth Flash space ahead of demand.

If productionized, this could bring Go's performance ceiling closer to Rust and C++ for compute-intensive workloads, broadening Go's applicability in AI and systems programming. ([Go Blog](https://go.dev/blog/simd-experiment))

### What to watch
