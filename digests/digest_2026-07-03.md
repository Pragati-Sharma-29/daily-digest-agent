## Deep Tech & Materials Science

### What happened
Asianometry published a deep dive into iron nitride magnets, a 40-year unsolved mystery in materials science. The original 1972 discovery of a potentially ultra-high-performance magnet that doesn't rely on rare earth elements has never been reliably reproduced, but the strategic stakes — reducing dependence on Chinese rare earth supply chains — have never been higher.

### Key stories
- **The 40-Year Mystery of the Iron Nitride Magnet** — In 1972, researchers reported iron nitride had the highest saturation magnetization at room temperature of any known material, which would make it ideal for motors, generators, and electronics — without requiring rare earth elements that China dominates. Asianometry explores why this discovery has never been reliably reproduced and what it would mean for global supply chains and the clean energy transition if the mystery were finally solved. ([Asianometry](https://asianometry.passport.online/member/episode/the-40-year-mystery-of-the-iron-nitride-magnet))

### What to watch
Given China's current rare earth export restrictions and their strategic implications for EV motors and wind turbines, any credible breakthrough in rare-earth-free magnet technology would be geopolitically significant. Watch for new research publications on iron nitride from labs funded by the US ARPA-E or equivalent European programs.
