## AI Security & Supply Chain Risk

### What happened
This week delivered two landmark demonstrations that the AI inference supply chain is fundamentally broken from a security standpoint. A canary-based research study proved that private user data leaks through AI systems at scale, while a months-long prompt theft operation at three separate AI hosting providers went completely undetected until external researchers alerted them. Separately, AI is being weaponized offensively: suspected Chinese hackers used autonomous AI agents to discover and exploit a zero-day in medical imaging software.

### Key stories
- **Researcher's 2 million canaries expose leaky AI supply chain** — A researcher planted 2 million uniquely trackable prompt "canaries" across AI systems and proved that private data consistently escapes intended data boundaries, surfacing in unexpected places. This is empirical proof of a systemic problem rather than an isolated incident, and has major implications for enterprises using shared AI infrastructure. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/))
- **OpenRouter drops 3 providers after months of prompt theft** — Dragos researchers discovered that three AI inference providers had been actively stealing user prompts for months, with none of the three providers aware until externally notified. OpenRouter terminated these providers upon disclosure — a stark illustration that the AI hosting market lacks basic security audit mechanisms. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/openrouter-drops-3-providers-after.html))
- **AI agents autonomously find and exploit medical imaging zero-day** — Suspected China-linked hackers deployed autonomous AI agents that discovered a previously unknown vulnerability in MONAI Label, an open-source medical imaging server, and used it to gain root access to a university server. This marks a qualitative escalation: AI is now being used not just to accelerate known exploits, but to discover novel vulnerabilities. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/attackers-ai-agents-found-zero-day-in-medical-imaging-software-and-gained-root-access.html))
- **Meta Muse hijacked via single malicious webpage** — Researchers showed that a single malicious webpage could extract a Meta Muse user's private conversations and stored memories without any user approval prompt. The attack could also modify the assistant's stored memories — a novel and deeply concerning attack vector for AI assistants that maintain persistent user context. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/a-single-malicious-webpage-let.html))

### What to watch
Regulatory pressure on AI hosting providers to implement data isolation standards is now almost inevitable given this week's disclosures. Watch for SOC 2 and similar audit frameworks to begin requiring AI-specific data isolation controls. The autonomous zero-day discovery case will likely accelerate government interest in restricting AI use in offensive cybersecurity.

- **Signal blocks Siri and Gemini specifically** — BleepingComputer reported that Signal's new protections explicitly target Siri and Gemini, leveraging iOS 27 APIs to block screenshot capture and Android controls to block AI assistant access. This creates a direct and named conflict between Signal's privacy model and Apple and Google's AI ambitions — a tension that will only grow. ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/signal-to-block-siri-gemini-ai-from-capturing-chats-on-ios-android/))

### What to watch
Watch for Apple and Google's responses — whether they provide OS-level APIs that allow apps like Signal to enforce these restrictions, or whether platform AI integration overrides app-level privacy controls. This is a defining regulatory and product battle for the next era of mobile AI.
