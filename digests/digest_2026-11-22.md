## AI Supply Chain Security & Model Evaluation

### What happened
Two significant AI infrastructure trust stories dominated this weekend: researchers exposed systematic data leakage from AI inference providers via planted "canary" prompts, and OpenAI published a detailed explainer on using perplexity as a shared metric for evaluating AI models. Together, these stories underscore that the AI supply chain — the pipes through which models are trained, hosted, and evaluated — remains poorly secured and poorly standardized.

### Key stories
- **Researcher plants 2 million canaries into AI supply chain, exposes mass data leakage** — A researcher seeded AI inference providers with unique, trackable "canary" prompts and found that user data was being exfiltrated at scale — a privacy disaster affecting customers who assumed their prompts stayed private. Dragos, the industrial cybersecurity firm, identified three specific providers; OpenRouter subsequently dropped all three. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/))
- **OpenRouter drops 3 providers after prompt theft discovery** — The incident is notable because none of the three affected providers were aware of the breach until researchers disclosed it, suggesting a systemic lack of audit and monitoring infrastructure across the third-party AI hosting industry. This should prompt serious enterprise customers to reassess their inference provider vetting processes. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/openrouter-drops-3-providers-after.html))
- **OpenAI publishes explainer on perplexity as an AI evaluation metric** — OpenAI's piece argues that perplexity — a measure of how well a model predicts a sample of text — could serve as a shared, rigorous metric for evaluating emerging AI capabilities, addressing the current fragmentation in how labs benchmark their models. Establishing shared evaluation standards is a prerequisite for meaningful AI governance. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence))

### What to watch
Regulatory bodies in the EU and US are increasingly focused on AI supply chain transparency — this canary research is exactly the kind of evidence that could accelerate mandatory audit requirements for AI inference providers. Watch for OpenAI to follow up its perplexity post with any proposed consortium or standards body engagement.

([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/attackers-ai-agents-found-zero-day-in-medical-imaging-software-and-gained-root-access.html))
- **China's "Armory Castle" exploits VMware vCenter zero-day** — A China-nexus threat group, while the last wave of VMware vCenter patches was still being applied, had already staged a new zero-day, suggesting persistent and sophisticated access to VMware's attack surface. ([Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/cyberattacks-data-breaches/china-armory-castle-vmware-vcenter-zero-day))
- **Microsoft Entra Connect Health flaw exploited to steal tenant data** — Attackers exploited a vulnerability in Microsoft's Entra Connect Health service to access administrator email addresses and tenant data, a particularly serious breach given Entra's role as identity infrastructure for enterprise Microsoft environments. ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/microsoft-entra-connect-health-flaw-exploited-to-steal-tenant-data/))
- **Rackspace Spot breach exposes Kubernetes secrets** — Hackers used compromised credentials to breach Rackspace's Spot cloud platform, deploy unauthorized workloads, and access Kubernetes secrets — highlighting how credential compromise in cloud management platforms can cascade into full infrastructure access. - **Ghostscript sandbox-escape RCE via crafted file uploads** — A now-patched flaw in Ghostscript allows crafted file uploads to escape the security sandbox and execute arbitrary commands on servers, affecting a wide range of Linux server applications that use Ghostscript for document processing. - **GnuPG warns of critical GPGME memory flaw via malicious OpenPGP keys** — A malicious OpenPGP key with an unusually long user ID can trigger a critical memory corruption bug in GPGME, the standard library used by applications to interact with GnuPG, potentially exposing sensitive encrypted data. ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/gnupg-warns-critical-gpgme-flaw-could.html))
- **CTO of "RansomwareHelp" indicted for wire fraud** — The co-founder and CTO of ransomwarehelp.com, a Florida company that claimed to decrypt ransomware victims' files, has been indicted for wire fraud — suggesting the firm was not actually decrypting data but pocketing recovery fees while deceiving victims. ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2026/11/cto-of-ransomwarehelp-indicted-for-wire-fraud/))
