## OpenAI's Jalapeño Inference Chip

### What happened
OpenAI and Broadcom jointly unveiled "Jalapeño," OpenAI's first custom silicon specifically designed for LLM inference workloads. The chip is purpose-built for OpenAI's unique inference needs, prioritizing performance, efficiency, and scale rather than the general-purpose training workloads that dominate Nvidia's GPU lineup.

### Key stories
- **OpenAI and Broadcom unveil Jalapeño inference chip** — This is a landmark moment for OpenAI, marking its transition from a pure software company into a vertically integrated AI stack player. Custom inference silicon gives OpenAI significant leverage over cost and performance at scale, reducing dependence on Nvidia and potentially enabling pricing advantages as inference demand grows. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/openai-broadcom-jalapeno-inference-chip))
- **TechCrunch details Jalapeño's design philosophy** — The chip was designed specifically around the unique memory access patterns and compute profiles of large language model inference, not general-purpose AI training. This distinction matters enormously: inference is now the dominant cost for deployed AI products, so optimizing for it directly addresses OpenAI's core operational expense. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/24/openai-unveils-its-first-custom-chip-built-by-broadcom/))

### What to watch
Watch for whether Jalapeño gets deployed in OpenAI's own data centers or is made available to third parties, and how Nvidia responds competitively. The broader question is whether this accelerates a trend of hyperscalers and AI labs building their own silicon, further pressuring Nvidia's dominance.

- **Stratechery: memory chips and China's rising threat** — Ben Thompson argues that the top three memory makers (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) may come to regret any moves that open the door to Chinese memory competitors, while also noting that Microsoft has strong commercial incentives to incorporate Chinese AI models into its products — a geopolitically charged and underappreciated dynamic. ([Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/memory-chips-and-china-microsoft-and-chinese-models/))
- **Microsoft's quantum computing claims questioned again** — A new critique published in Nature casts fresh doubt on Microsoft's topological qubit approach, potentially threatening its 2029 commercial quantum target. While rivals pursue more established superconducting methods, Microsoft's long-term bet on a fundamentally different architecture remains scientifically contested. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/microsofts-quantum-computing-technology-called-into-question-again/articleshow/131971788.cms))
- **AMD reinstates memory encryption in consumer CPUs** — Following significant user backlash, AMD reversed a decision to remove memory encryption from consumer-grade CPUs — a move critics had seen as a ploy to push users toward more expensive enterprise chips. The reversal signals that hardware makers are not immune to community pressure on security features. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/06/following-user-outcry-amd-reinstates-memory-encryption-in-consumer-cpus/))

### What to watch
The TSMC capacity crunch is the central tension in the global AI buildout. Watch for further announcements of alternative manufacturing partnerships and for U.S. policy responses to Chinese memory makers gaining share in a strategically critical sector.

## Cybersecurity & Post-Quantum Cryptography

### What happened
The U.S. government dramatically accelerated its post-quantum cryptography migration timeline via executive order, while Mandiant disclosed active exploitation of a zero-day in Cisco's SD-WAN infrastructure — a combination that paints an urgent picture of evolving threat landscapes for enterprise and government security teams.

### Key stories
- **White House shortens post-quantum cryptography deadline** — An executive order has moved up the federal deadline to migrate off quantum-vulnerable cryptographic standards, citing national security risks from adversaries with advancing quantum capabilities. This creates immediate pressure on government contractors and critical infrastructure operators to accelerate PQC adoption. ([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2026/06/executive-order-bumps-up-deadline-to-move-off-quantum-vulnerable-crypto/))
- **Zero-day exploited in Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager** — Mandiant identified a threat actor actively exploiting a zero-day vulnerability (CVE-2026-20245) in Cisco's SD-WAN Manager, targeting service provider infrastructure. SD-WAN is widely deployed in enterprise and telecom environments, making this a high-severity finding with broad exposure. ([Google Cloud / Mandiant](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/zero-day-exploitation-cisco-catalyst-sd-wan-manager/))
- **a16z crypto leads $33M round in compute marketplace Ornn** — Founded by MIT graduates, Ornn aims to bring transparency and efficiency to the opaque compute procurement market. The a16z crypto involvement suggests a blockchain or token-based settlement layer for compute resources, an interesting structural bet on decentralized infrastructure markets. ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/funding/a16z-crypto-leads-33-million-bet-on-compute-marketplace-ornn/articleshow/131971804.cms))

### What to watch
The compressed post-quantum deadline will force a wave of cryptographic inventory audits and migrations across government and enterprise. Watch for NIST PQC standard implementations to accelerate and for a new category of migration tooling companies to emerge.
