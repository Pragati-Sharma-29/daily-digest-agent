## Cybersecurity & AI Risks

### What happened
The cybersecurity landscape is increasingly complex, with new attack vectors emerging, including those targeting GPUs and exploiting vulnerabilities in AI agentic tools. The threat posed by quantum computing to current encryption standards is accelerating, forcing a re-evaluation of security timelines. While AI is seen as a long-term solution, it introduces short-term risks that demand immediate attention, with identity emerging as a major failure point in enterprise security.

### Key stories
- **OpenClaw Gives Users Yet Another Reason to Be Freaked Out About Security** — The viral AI agentic tool, OpenClaw, has been identified as a significant security risk, allowing attackers to silently gain unauthenticated administrator access. This vulnerability underscores the inherent dangers of rapidly deployed AI tools without sufficient security vetting, potentially exposing sensitive systems to compromise. ([Ars_Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/04/heres-why-its-prudent-for-openclaw-users-to-assume-compromise/))
- **New Rowhammer Attacks Give Complete Control of Machines Running Nvidia GPUs** — Researchers have discovered novel Rowhammer attacks, including GDDRHammer, GeForge, and GPUBreach, which can manipulate GPU memory to effectively hijack the CPU and gain complete control over a machine. This development highlights a critical new vulnerability in hardware that could have profound implications for data integrity and system security. ([Ars_Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/04/new-rowhammer-attacks-give-complete-control-of-machines-running-nvidia-gpus/))
- **Google Bumps Up Q Day Deadline to 2029, Accelerating the Quantum Threat to Encryption** — Google has revised its estimate for "Q Day" – the point at which quantum computers will be capable of breaking vital encryption – to 2029, significantly sooner than previously anticipated. This accelerated timeline serves as a stark warning to the entire industry to hasten the transition away from vulnerable RSA and Elliptic Curve cryptosystems. ([Ars_Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/03/google-bumps-up-q-day-estimate-to-2029-far-sooner-than-previously-thought/))
- **Linx Security Raises $50M Series B as Identity Becomes Security’s Biggest Failure Point** — Linx Security secured $50 million in Series B funding to fuel its global expansion, focusing on addressing identity-related weaknesses which are increasingly implicated in security breaches. The company's platform provides enterprises with real-time visibility and control over every identity within their environment, a crucial capability as identity becomes a primary attack vector. ([Insight_Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/ideas/linx-security-raises-50m-series-b-as-identity-becomes-securitys-biggest-failure-point/))
- **AI and Security: Short-Term Pain for Long-Term Gain?** — Experts suggest that while AI will initially be detrimental to cybersecurity by enabling more sophisticated attacks, it is expected to become a superior defense mechanism compared to human efforts in the long term. This outlook implies a challenging transitional period where organizations must bolster their defenses against AI-powered threats. ([Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/2026/axios-supply-chain-attack-claude-code-code-leaked-ai-and-security/))

The escalating arms race between AI-powered attacks and defenses will continue to drive innovation in cybersecurity. Organizations must prioritize robust identity management and prepare for quantum-resistant cryptography, as well as address new hardware-level vulnerabilities.
